Pseudomonas aeruginosa quick-start (strains, PA14 library, genetics)

Orientation for P. aeruginosa work: biosafety, common strains, the PA14 non-redundant transposon library and its caveats, and what to check before starting genetics.

About this page

This page is an orientation for working with Pseudomonas aeruginosa. It has not yet been run in this lab, so follow a published protocol for your strain. Highlighted text has not been checked against a source.

Biosafety

P. aeruginosa is normally handled at biosafety level 2. Check your institution’s rules and the risk assessment for your strain.

Common strains

  • PA14 is a clinical isolate from a burn patient that is pathogenic in several model hosts and in mice (PA14 resource site).
  • PAO1 is the other widely used reference strain. The two differ in genome content and virulence, so state which one you used and do not assume results transfer.

The PA14 non-redundant transposon library

  • The PA14NR set contains 5,459 mutants (version 1.0), drawn from a parental library of 38,976 mutants, and covers 4,596 predicted PA14 genes, about 77% of the 5,962 predicted (Liberati et al. 2006).
  • The main transposon is MAR2xT7, a mariner-family derivative of Himar1. Those mutants were selected on gentamicin (15 µg/mL). Some mutants carry TnphoA instead, selected on neomycin and maintained on kanamycin (Liberati et al. 2006).
  • Caveats (Liberati et al. 2006):
    • Coverage is incomplete, with insertion-site hot spots and some genes not targeted.
    • The list of 335 candidate essential genes may include non-essential genes.
    • Insertions near the 5′ end of a gene can give functional fusion proteins, and insertions can affect downstream genes.
    • About 2.8% of mutants are expected to be mislabeled. In a test of 109 mutants, 106 had the expected insertion.
    • Small colony variants arose under static growth, so culture conditions were adjusted.
  • Good practice: verify the insertion by PCR before relying on a mutant, and complement it. See also Tn-seq data analysis for pooled screens with mariner-type transposons.

Culture and genetics

  1. Grow on LB at 37 °C. P. aeruginosa grows quickly and is often recognized by its smell and by green pigment.
  2. P. aeruginosa is intrinsically resistant to many antibiotics, so antibiotic concentrations used for E. coli will not necessarily work. Check published concentrations for your strain and marker.
  3. Gene deletions are commonly made by conjugation from an E. coli donor with a suicide plasmid and sacB sucrose counter-selection. Use a published protocol for your strain. The bi-parental conjugation page is a model for the donor/counter-selection design only, since it was written for a different recipient.
