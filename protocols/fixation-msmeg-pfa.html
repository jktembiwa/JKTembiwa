Fixing M. smegmatis cells with paraformaldehyde for microscopy

Quick PFA fixation for trial imaging, with warnings about artefacts.

Draft: not yet reviewed by the author Draft 0.1

Highlighted values are awaiting confirmation. Do not rely on them without checking.

Warning: fixation can alter cell shape and the distribution of fluorophore-tagged molecules. For a final experiment, test that fixation does not affect the phenomenon you are studying. Fixation is best for trial runs or when live imaging is not possible. Do not leave fixed cells in the fridge for more than one night; they fall apart.

Requirements: PBS-Tween (1× PBS + 0.05% Tween 80) and 16% PFA (a handling and storage guide is in your institution’s chemical safety rules; PFA is toxic, work in a hood).

  1. Pellet the culture (5,000 rpm, 5 min, clinical centrifuge). Discard supernatant.
  2. Resuspend in 1 mL PBS-Tween and move to a labeled microcentrifuge tube.
  3. Add 100 µL 16% PFA (about 1.5% final). Invert to mix.
  4. Leave 10 min on the bench.
  5. Spin about 1 min at maximum speed in a microcentrifuge.
  6. Resuspend in 1 mL PBS-Tween.
  7. Keep at 4 °C until imaging (no more than one night).

For probe-labeled cells see Probe labeling; image settings in HADA/NADA staining.
