Inducible expression and depletion systems for mycobacteria

Comparison of tetracycline, nitrile and acetamide induction systems and two depletion strategies.

Draft: not yet reviewed by the author Draft 0.1

Highlighted values are awaiting confirmation. Do not rely on them without checking.

Inducible expression

Tet-ON (anhydrotetracycline, aTc). aTc turns expression on of genes cloned downstream of the TetO promoter. Maximal induction is at about 100 ng/mL. aTc is light sensitive; cover tubes with foil for storage and time courses. One-plasmid systems use hygromycin with pUVTetOR or pTetSG; two-plasmid systems combine hygromycin and kanamycin with pTetO and pMC1. The system is used with doxycycline as inducer in mice.

Nitrile (pNIT). Caprolactam or isovaleronitrile (IVN) turn on genes downstream of the NitA-dependent promoter via the NitA activator. A single-plasmid system. M. tuberculosis uses caprolactam; M. smegmatis is induced with IVN at a 10⁻⁶ or 10⁻⁷ dilution of a main stock (about 1–10 µM). NitA is somewhat toxic: cells filament a little and grow slowly after induction. Although published as equivalent, pNIT induces less than the Tet system.

Acetamide. The inducer is acetamide, an amide, acting on the acetamidase promoter. A single-plasmid system. There is conflicting evidence for leaky expression from the uninduced promoter.

Depletion

Tet promoter replacement. The native promoter is replaced by an aTc-inducible promoter, commonly by homologous recombination or recombineering. The gene is depleted by diluting the strain into medium without aTc.

Inducible degradation. The target is tagged with a degradation tag that is cleaved by an inducible HIV-2 protease, uncovering an ssrA signal recognized by Clp proteases. Adding aTc leads to target degradation.
