Klebsiella pneumoniae quick-start (strains, culture, typing)

Orientation for working with K. pneumoniae: biosafety, the capsule and hypermucoviscosity, genome-based typing with Kleborate, and what to check before starting genetics.

About this page

This page is an orientation for working with Klebsiella pneumoniae. It has not yet been run in this lab, and most of it is general knowledge that has not been checked against a source, so highlighted text should be confirmed before use. For anything quantitative, follow a published protocol for your strain.

Biosafety

K. pneumoniae is normally handled at biosafety level 2. Check your institution’s rules and the risk assessment for your strain, particularly for hypervirulent or carbapenem-resistant isolates.

Know your strain first

  • K. pneumoniae strains differ widely. Classical, hypervirulent and multidrug-resistant lineages behave differently, so identify the sequence type (ST), capsule (K) type and resistance genes before choosing antibiotics or a transformation method.
  • Kleborate is a genotyping tool for K. pneumoniae and its related species complex, described in Lam et al. (2021) as a genomic surveillance framework. It takes an assembled genome and reports the species, ST, virulence factors, resistance genes and capsule type. Run it before you plan selection markers. See whole-genome sequencing analysis for assembling a genome.

Culture

  1. Grow on LB or similar rich medium at 37 °C. Overnight cultures and colonies the next day are normal.
  2. Check the strain’s antibiotic susceptibility before picking a selection marker, because many clinical isolates are resistant to common antibiotics, and K. pneumoniae is intrinsically resistant to ampicillin. Working concentrations for E. coli are in the antibiotic table, but they may not apply to Klebsiella.

Capsule and hypermucoviscosity

  • The capsule is a major virulence factor and makes colonies glossy and mucoid.
  • The string test is a quick check for hypermucoviscosity: touch a colony with a loop and lift. A string longer than about 5 mm is usually called positive. It is a phenotype, not a proof of hypervirulence.
  • A thick capsule can lower transformation efficiency and make cells difficult to pellet. Expect to need to optimize.

Genetics

Plasmid-based λ Red recombineering and conjugation from E. coli donors have been used in Klebsiella. Find a published protocol for your strain before starting, and use the bi-parental conjugation page only as a model for the donor/counter-selection design, since that protocol was written for a different recipient.

Sources
