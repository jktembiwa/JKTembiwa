Standardizing culture samples for assays

Reduce day-to-day variance by freezing standardized aliquots of single-cell suspensions at a defined OD.

Version 1.0

Use this when day-to-day or other variance is a problem in assays such as kill curves, growth curves or screens.

  1. Grow the strain to log phase.
  2. Sonicate or filter the culture to make single-cell suspensions. This is not always necessary for M. smegmatis but is necessary for M. tuberculosis.
  3. Measure the OD of the sonicated or filtered culture.
  4. Dilute and make aliquots (200 µL to 1 mL per cryovial is fine) at an OD set by your desired starter-culture OD. For example, to start a 10 mL starter at OD 0.1, make 1 mL aliquots at OD 1.
  5. For short-term storage (less than a week) store aliquots at 4°C in a refrigerator. For long-term storage (over a week), add equal volume 50% glycerol to aliquots and store at -80°C. When adding glycerol, make sure to double the OD of the aliquot prior and based on the desired resuspension OD (e.g. make 1 mL aliquots at OD 2).
  6. Freeze the aliquots. Some cells are lost on freeze–thaw, but the loss should be the same for each tube if you thaw them the same way each time.
  7. Subculture from an aliquot and run the assay when the culture reaches the OD you want. Sonicate before plating or seeding M. tuberculosis.
