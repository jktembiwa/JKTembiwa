Ethidium bromide permeability / efflux assay (M. smegmatis)

Measure ethidium bromide uptake over time in a plate reader, with or without antibiotic pre-treatment.

Draft: not yet reviewed by the author Draft 0.1

Highlighted values are awaiting confirmation. Do not rely on them without checking.

Day 1

Set up overnight cultures in 5 mL 7H9, diluted so that they reach OD₆₀₀ ~0.6 by the planned morning time.

Day 2

  1. Take 1 mL culture. Pellet (16,000 × g, 1 min, room temperature) and wash twice in PBS + 0.05% Tween 80. Resuspend in 1 mL PBS + Tween 80.
  2. Add ethidium bromide to 2 µg/mL.
  3. In a black 96-well plate, add 4.5 µL nanopure water to every well that will receive culture.
  4. Add 195.5 µL bacterial suspension in triplicate (200 µL final). Track the wells carefully.
  5. Plate reader: 37 °C, no shaking, excitation 520 nm, emission 560–640 nm. Read every 60 s for 90 min.
  6. Express fluorescence as the percent increase over each sample’s value at 1 min.
  7. 2 µg/mL EtBr should be below the MIC for M. smegmatis and should not itself reduce viability. If a mutant might be hypersusceptible, determine the EtBr MIC first.

Permeability with an antibiotic (example: meropenem)

  1. Thaw a 10 mg/mL meropenem stock and vortex.
  2. Overnight cultures at OD ~0.4. Dilute to OD 0.1 in 10 mL fresh 7H9.
  3. Prepare two sets, (+) and (−) antibiotic. Add meropenem at ½ MIC of the wild-type strain: value to be filled in to (+). Incubate at 37 °C on a roller.
  4. Run the permeability assay (steps 1–6) at 0, 3 and 6 h. At each time point take 1 mL, wash three times, normalize all resuspensions to the same OD (measure each time), and scale the EtBr volume to the final resuspension volume.

Alternative settings (filter-based reader)

Excitation filter 530 nm, emission filter 590 nm, 570 nm dichroic mirror; black 96-well plate; no shaking; room temperature; 30 min duration; reading every 15 s.
