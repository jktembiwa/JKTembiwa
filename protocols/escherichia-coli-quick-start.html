Escherichia coli quick-start (strains, deletion collections, λ Red)

Orientation for E. coli genetics: the Keio deletion collection and its caveats, and the one-step λ Red method for making your own deletions.

Study notes: not yet run by the author Version 0.1

Highlighted values are awaiting confirmation. Do not rely on them without checking.

About this page

This page summarizes the Keio collection and the Datsenko–Wanner deletion method for E. coli K-12. It has not yet been run in this lab, so follow the original papers for exact conditions. Highlighted text has not been checked against a source. Routine procedures are on other pages: chemical transformation, electrocompetent cells, recombineering, colony PCR and antibiotic plates.

Biosafety

Laboratory K-12 strains are normally handled at biosafety level 1. Pathogenic E. coli (for example enterohaemorrhagic strains) are not, so check the risk assessment.

The Keio collection

  • Single-gene in-frame deletions in E. coli K-12 strain BW25113: 3,985 non-essential gene deletion mutants, from 4,288 genes targeted. About 299 open reading frames were identified as likely essential (Baba et al. 2006; Keio Collection summary).
  • Each gene was replaced with a kanamycin-resistance cassette by the λ Red method (Keio Collection summary), so mutants are selected on kanamycin. The cassette is flanked by FRT sites and can be removed with FLP recombinase.
  • Caveats (Keio Collection summary):
    • Spontaneous mutations, including frameshifts, can arise in a stock and be wrongly attributed to the deleted gene.
    • Deletions can have polar effects on downstream genes in the same operon.
    • Phenotypes can depend on growth conditions.
    • A later check found 37 strains incorrect because of gene duplications.
  • Good practice: verify the deletion by colony PCR, and move it into a clean background by P1 transduction before drawing conclusions. Complement with the gene on a plasmid.

One-step deletion with λ Red (Datsenko and Wanner 2000)

The method replaces a chromosomal gene with a PCR-made resistance cassette flanked by short homology arms. It uses plasmids that are described in the paper: template plasmids carrying the resistance cassettes (pKD3 and pKD4), a λ Red helper plasmid (pKD46) and an FLP plasmid (pCP20).

  1. Make a PCR product from pKD3 or pKD4 using primers with about 40 nt of homology to the target at the 5′ ends.
  2. Transform the strain carrying pKD46, which has a temperature-sensitive replicon and an arabinose-inducible λ Red. Grow at 30 °C with ampicillin and induce with L-arabinose before making cells electrocompetent.
  3. Electroporate the PCR product (see electrocompetent cells) and select recombinants on the cassette’s antibiotic.
  4. Confirm the deletion by colony PCR with primers outside the target.
  5. To remove the cassette, transform with pCP20 (FLP recombinase, temperature-sensitive) and cure the plasmid by growing at a higher temperature.
