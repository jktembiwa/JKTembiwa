Moenomycin treatment assays (kill curve, growth curve, microscopy) with MurJ depletion

Three assay formats for testing the effect of moenomycin on mycobacteria in which a target (here MurJ) is depleted with anhydrotetracycline.

Draft: not yet reviewed by the author Draft 0.1

Highlighted values are awaiting confirmation. Do not rely on them without checking.

Moenomycin is a transglycosylase (PBP1-type) inhibitor. These assays combine it with anhydrotetracycline (aTc)-induced depletion of a target, so that drug sensitivity can be compared with and without depletion. See Induction and depletion systems.

Kill curve (CFU)

  1. Treat cells with 100 µg/mL moenomycin and 500 ng/mL aTc.
  2. Take time points every 15 min for the first hour, then every 30 min up to 2 h.
  3. Serially dilute and plate each time point for CFU (Serial dilution and CFU). Take the zero time point before adding drug.

Growth curve

  1. Treat cells with 2–3 concentrations of moenomycin (20, 50 and 100 µg/mL) and 500 ng/mL aTc, alongside no-drug and no-aTc controls.
  2. Run a plate-reader growth curve (Growth curve setup) for up to 8 h.

Microscopy

  1. Add aTc first to start depletion.
  2. At each depletion time point (4, 8, 14 and 20 h): add 100 µg/mL moenomycin and roll 45 min, then add HADA and roll 15 min more at 37 °C. Wash and fix with 16% paraformaldehyde (final concentration to be confirmed).
  3. Image within 24 h of fixation.

Plate, vehicle and fixation details to be confirmed by the author.
