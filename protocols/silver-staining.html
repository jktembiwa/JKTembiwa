Silver staining of SDS-PAGE gels

Fix, sensitize, stain with silver nitrate, develop and quench; sensitive to about 0.02 µg protein.

Version 1.0

Wear gloves (contamination, and the chemicals are hazardous). Handle the gel gently; pressure creates artifacts. Use about 5 gel volumes of each solution. Make the sensitizing, silver nitrate and developing solutions fresh; fixing and quenching solutions can be made ahead. Use only ultra-pure MilliQ water, as tap or ionized water will leave brown-black background.

  1. Fix in fixing solution at least 2 h or overnight, at room temperature, with gentle shaking.
  2. Ethanol washes: 30% ethanol, 20 min, gentle shaking; repeat once (removes detergents and salts).
  3. Sensitize: use fresh sensitizing solution for only 2 min. Wash 3 × 30 s with water.
  4. Stain: silver nitrate staining solution (about 100 mg silver nitrate works well), 20 min, gentle shaking. Wash 3 × 15 s with deionized water. Do not wash longer or more times; too much washing removes the silver and gives artifacts.
  5. Develop: developing solution at room temperature, watching closely. Most gels take 2–7 min; up to 15 min for low protein amounts. Stop as soon as the desired contrast is reached and discard the solution immediately. Long development gives high background. In our hands ~0.02 µg protein was visible in the first 1–2 min.
  6. Quench: 50 mL 1% acetic acid for a few minutes. A quick rinse with water is enough for an image; extra water washes were not found to help.

Solution recipes (reference set)

The original protocol points to Green and Sambrook for its recipes. The Green and Sambrook recipes were not available to me, so the set below is the published Chevallet, Luche and Rabilloud (2006) formaldehyde/thiosulfate silver nitrate protocol, whose steps are close to the ones above.

Solution Recipe
Fixative 30% ethanol, 10% glacial acetic acid
Wash 20% ethanol, then water
Sensitizer 0.8 mM sodium thiosulfate (0.02% w/v if using the pentahydrate)
Silver nitrate Use 1.0 g of silver nitrate per 1 L of ultra-pure water, however make fresh the day-of in 100 mL batches and store in foil-covered autoclave glass bottles and keep in the dark). You can make 100 or 10 mg aliquots of silver nitrate in epp tubes and store them in a foil covered tip box for easy sol’n preparation.
Developer (make fresh) 3% potassium carbonate, 250 µL formalin per liter, 125 µL of 10% sodium thiosulfate per liter; add formalin ≤1 h before use
Stop 4% (w/v) Tris, 2% (v/v) glacial acetic acid (40 g Tris + 20 mL acetic acid per liter); the protocol above quenches in 1% acetic acid instead

In the published version: fix ≥30 min; wash 2 × 10 min in 20% ethanol, 2 × 10 min in water; sensitize 1 min; rinse 2 × 1 min in water; silver 20 min–2 h; dip 10 s in water; develop (strongest bands appear in seconds, don’t let develop for more than 2 minutes to prevent artefacts); stop ≥30 min.

For mass spectrometry downstream, a single 30 min fix gives the best sequence coverage, and aldehyde-free silver-ammonia variants, plus a ferricyanide–thiosulfate destain of excised bands, are described in the same paper.

Reference: Chevallet M, Luche S, Rabilloud T. Silver staining of proteins in polyacrylamide gels. Nat Protoc 2006;1:1852–1858. Free preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.4396
