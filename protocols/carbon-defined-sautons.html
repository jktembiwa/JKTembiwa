Carbon-defined Sauton's medium

Sauton's medium modified so that the carbon source is defined and set by you, with 20% carbon-source stocks.

Draft: not yet reviewed by the author Draft 0.1

Highlighted values are awaiting confirmation. Do not rely on them without checking.

Medium

Ingredient 1 L 500 mL 250 mL
KH₂PO₄ 0.5 g 0.25 g 0.125 g
MgSO₄·7H₂O 0.5 g 0.25 g 0.125 g
Citric acid (see note 1) 2.0 g 1.0 g 0.5 g
Ferric ammonium citrate 0.05 g 0.025 g 0.0125 g
(NH₄)₂SO₄ (see note 2) 0.5 g 0.25 g 0.125 g
ZnSO₄, 1% solution 0.1 mL 0.05 mL 0.025 mL
Carbon source, 20% solution (see note 3) 10–40 mL 5–20 mL 2.5–10 mL
Tyloxapol, 10% solution 5 mL 2.5 mL 1.25 mL
ddH₂O to 1000 mL to 500 mL to 250 mL
  1. Dissolve the salts in warm distilled water, about one-third of the final volume.
  2. Add the carbon source and tyloxapol, and bring to volume with ddH₂O.
  3. Adjust the pH to about 7.4, then filter sterilize.

Optionally, make the Sauton’s base without carbon source and add filter-sterilized carbon source as desired.

Notes

  1. Previous experiments suggest that M. tuberculosis cannot use citric acid as a carbon source.
  2. Ammonium sulfate replaces asparagine, because M. tuberculosis can use asparagine as a carbon source. The 0.5 g/L concentration is based on 7H9 medium.
  3. The optimal final carbon-source concentration depends on the experiment. For a single carbon source use 0.2–0.4% glucose or glycerol (0.4% glucose gives more robust growth than 0.2%), or 0.2% acetate or fatty acids. Propionate and valerate may need to be reduced to 0.1%, since toxicity is sometimes seen at 0.2%. When combining two carbon sources, use 0.1% of each.

20% carbon-source stocks (filter sterilize)

Stock Preparation
Glucose 1 g, brought to 5 mL with ddH₂O
Acetate 1 g sodium acetate (C₂H₃NaO₂), brought to 5 mL with ddH₂O
Glycerol 1 mL, brought to 5 mL with ddH₂O
Propionic acid 1 mL, brought to 5 mL with ddH₂O
Butyric acid 1 mL, brought to 5 mL with ddH₂O. It smells strongly: prepare in a chemical hood and dispose of waste in a closed container.
Valeric acid (C₅H₁₀O₂) 1 mL, brought to 5 mL with 50% ethanol
Hexanoic acid (C₆H₁₂O₂) 1 mL, brought to 5 mL with 50% ethanol

The ethanol carried over with the fatty acids is small relative to the fatty acid, and slow-growing mycobacteria seem unable to metabolize ethanol (Pandey and Sassetti, PNAS 2008; Tsukamura, Tubercle 1988). The source notes a final ethanol concentration of 0.02% for 0.1% fatty acid; by my calculation it is closer to 0.2%. Please check.

Source / credit: Sauton's formula from Allen (1998), Methods in Molecular Biology 101 (Mycobacteria Protocols, Parish and Stoker, eds), p. 22; modified to be carbon-defined. Adapted by K. Tembiwa.
