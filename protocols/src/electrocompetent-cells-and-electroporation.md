## E. coli electrocompetent cells

1. Grow a starter culture. It can include the antibiotic to which the strain is resistant to select out contaminants (for XL1-Blue, tetracycline at 10 µg/mL).
2. Dilute to an OD of at least 0.05 in LB without antibiotic. Grow to an OD of about 0.8 (up to 1.0 is fine).
3. Spin at 5,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C and pour off the supernatant.
4. Wash twice in ice-cold sterile **10% glycerol**: first in half the original volume, then in a quarter. Keep the cells cold.
5. Resuspend the final pellet in ice-cold 10% glycerol to a final volume of one-hundredth the original (for a 200 mL culture, use 1.5–1.75 mL glycerol to make 2 mL). Aliquot 50 µL.
6. Use fresh, or snap-freeze for 30 seconds in liquid nitrogen (or a dry ice/95% ethanol bath) and store at −80 °C.

## M. smegmatis electrocompetent cells

Cells made this way have an efficiency of about 10⁴–10⁵ CFU/µg DNA. One vial can be split for up to 4 transformations.

1. Use a log-phase culture (10 mL is ideal). Pour into a 15 mL conical tube. Keep cells and 10% glycerol on ice.
2. Spin at 5,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C.
3. Remove the supernatant and resuspend in 10 mL 10% glycerol. Resuspending first in 1 mL with a pipette and then adding 9 mL saves supplies. Vortex briefly; keep the cells cold.
4. Spin again at 5,000 × g for 10 min. Repeat the glycerol wash 2–3 times in total. **Wash in 10% glycerol, not water.**
5. Resuspend the final pellet in 200 µL 10% glycerol.
6. Add 1 µL vector to 200 µL cells in a cuvette and electroporate (settings below). The desired pulse time is about 3 ms.
7. Keep on ice while preparing 1 mL of 7H9, then recover in 7H9 for 2–3 hours.
8. Spin at 14,000 × g for 30–60 seconds, remove the supernatant, resuspend in 200 µL 7H9, and plate on LB with antibiotic using beads.

## BCG and M. tuberculosis

- Make competent cells fresh each time to prevent arcing, following the *M. smegmatis* procedure.
- Resuspend at the end in 8/100 of the starting volume (for a 100 mL culture, 8 mL, not 1 mL) and use 400 µL of cells per transformation.
- Use a **different electroporation setting** from *M. smegmatis* and *E. coli*.

## Electroporator settings

| Organism | Voltage | Resistance | Capacitance |
|---|---|---|---|
| *E. coli* and *M. smegmatis* (standard transformation) | 2500 V | 125 Ω | 25 µF |
| BCG | 2500 V | 725 Ω | 50 µF |
| *M. smegmatis* recombineering (linear DNA) | 2500 V | 1000 Ω | 25 µF |

Standard transformation and recombineering use different resistances. For standard transformation the desired pulse time is about 3 ms; for recombineering the time constant should be 20–30 ms.

## Where it usually fails

- **Arcing.** Make BCG/*M. tuberculosis* competent cells fresh each time.
- **Low efficiency.** Wash in glycerol, not water, for mycobacteria, and keep E. coli cells cold throughout.
