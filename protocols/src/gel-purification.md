## Purpose

Recover a PCR product or digested vector from an agarose gel for cloning. Expect to lose a large fraction of the DNA: about 80–90% in kit-based gel purification in this lab's experience.

## Option 1: low-melt agarose (fast, works well for Gibson assembly)

1. Pour a 1% TAE gel with low-melt agarose instead of regular agarose. It takes about 2 hours to set at room temperature, so you may want to put the rig in the fridge after pouring.
2. Run your vector and inserts and cut out the bands. Handle the gel carefully; it falls apart more easily than a regular gel.
3. Warm the slices for a few minutes at 60 °C, vortex, and pipette 1–2 µL of the melted gel solution directly into the tube with the Gibson enzymes.

## Option 2: column kit (for example Promega Wizard SV Gel and PCR Clean-Up)

1. Cut out the gel slice and add Membrane Binding Solution.
2. Incubate at 50–60 °C for 2–3 hours, vortexing for 1–2 minutes every 30 minutes until the gel is fully dissolved.
3. Resuspend, transfer the dissolved gel to the minicolumn and leave it on the bench for 10 minutes.
4. Follow the kit protocol for the remaining steps.
5. To elute, add 30–50 µL elution buffer or nuclease-free water to the filter, leave 10 minutes, and centrifuge at 16,000 × g for 1 minute.

## Where it usually fails

- [**Very low yield.** Expected losses are high; the low-melt method is faster for Gibson assembly, and eluting in 30 µL rather than 50 µL gives a more concentrated product.]{.tbc}
- **Gel slice not dissolving.** Keep vortexing at the stated intervals during the 2–3 hour incubation.
