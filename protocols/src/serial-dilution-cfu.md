## Purpose

Estimate the number of viable cells (colony-forming units, CFU) per mL of a culture by diluting it until individual colonies can be counted on plates.

## Materials

- Culture to be counted
- Sterile diluent: PBS or 7H9 for mycobacteria, PBS or saline for *E. coli* / *S. aureus*. Add 0.05% Tween 80 for mycobacteria to limit clumping.
- Sterile 1.5 mL tubes (or a 96-well plate for multichannel work)
- Agar plates (7H10/7H11 + supplements for mycobacteria; LB or TSA for *E. coli* / *S. aureus*)
- Fresh pipette tips for every transfer

## Procedure: tenfold dilutions

1. Label one tube per dilution (10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁷, or as needed) and add 900 µL diluent to each.
2. Mix the culture well (vortex, and for mycobacteria pipette up and down to break clumps).
3. Transfer 100 µL of culture into the first tube (1:10, 10⁻¹). Mix thoroughly.
4. With a fresh tip, transfer 100 µL from the 10⁻¹ tube into the next tube (10⁻²). Mix. Continue down the series with a fresh tip each time.
5. Plate each dilution you need (see below). Include a no-culture diluent control.
6. Incubate and count (see table).

**Shortcut dilutions:** 1:9 means 1 part sample + 9 parts diluent (a tenfold dilution). 1:99 means 1 part + 99 parts (a 100-fold dilution), e.g. 10 µL + 990 µL.

## Plating

**Spread plating:** spread 100 µL of a dilution over the plate. Count plates with 30–300 colonies.

**Drop plating (saves plates):** spot 10 µL per dilution in a row on a plate (3 spots per dilution is typical). Let the drops dry before inverting. Count the spots with roughly 3–30 colonies.

## Counting and calculation

$$\text{CFU/mL} = \frac{\text{colonies counted}}{\text{volume plated (mL)} \times \text{dilution}}$$

Example: 42 colonies from 100 µL (0.1 mL) of the 10⁻⁵ dilution: 42 / (0.1 × 10⁻⁵) = 4.2 × 10⁷ CFU/mL.

Where several dilutions give countable plates, calculate each and report the mean.

## Incubation by organism

| | *E. coli* | *S. aureus* | *M. smegmatis* | BCG / *M. tuberculosis* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature | 37 °C | 37 °C | 37 °C | 37 °C |
| Time to countable colonies | overnight | [overnight (16–24 h)]{.tbc} | [3–4 days]{.tbc} | [3–4 weeks]{.tbc} |
| Plate medium | LB | [TSA / LB]{.tbc} | 7H10/7H11 or LB | 7H10/7H11 + OADC |

Seal mycobacterial plates in a bag so they do not dry out over long incubations. Work with BSL-2 organisms in a biosafety cabinet.

## Tips

- Always use a fresh tip between dilutions; carry-over inflates the counts at the high dilutions.
- Mix each tube before transferring. Settling or clumping is the biggest source of variation, especially in mycobacteria.
- Plate in duplicate or triplicate.
- For time-course killing assays, take the zero time point before adding drug and plate it at a dilution that can still be counted.
- If plates have no colonies at the lowest dilution you plated, report the result as below the limit of detection (one colony ÷ volume plated), not as zero.
