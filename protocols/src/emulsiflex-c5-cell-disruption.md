## Parts (letters used in the steps)

A air-supply hose and valve, B inlet air-pressure gauge, C discharge tubing, D hose with control valve for the pump motor (green knob), E hose to the sample cylinder, F regulator for pneumatic control (grey knob), G sample cylinder body, H sample cylinder cap, I pump motor, J pump body, K inlet check valve, L outlet check valve, M homogenizing valve, N pneumatic control gauge, O homogenizing pressure gauge, P cooling metal loop.

## Procedure

1. **Pre-cool your buffer** before the experiment.
2. Open the sample cylinder cap (H) and turn on the air supply (orange air knob, A). The cylinder (G) is filled with isopropanol. Put the free end of the discharge tubing (C) into a clean bottle to collect it. The collected isopropanol can be reused to make SDS-PAGE gels.
3. **Pump out the isopropanol.** Start the pump motor by slowly turning the green control valve knob (D) to vertical (the more you turn it, the more air is supplied). Watch the cylinder and leave just enough liquid to cover the inlet valve, so no air bubbles enter. Turn the pump off quickly by turning the knob horizontal. If a bubble gets in, turn the valve off, add water or buffer (whichever step you are on), turn it back on, and let the bubble leave through the discharge tubing.
4. **Rinse with water, then buffer.** Add about 250 mL of distilled water to the cylinder, put the discharge tube into a waste container, and repeat step 3. Then add about 100 mL of the buffer your sample will be homogenized in to equilibrate the instrument.
5. **Cool the system.** Add about 100 mL buffer, put the discharge tubing back into the cylinder to make a circulation loop, and place the cooling loop (P) in ice water. Circulate for about 5 min, then send the buffer to waste (repeat step 3).
6. **Load the sample** into the cylinder and start the pump. Discard the remaining buffer until your sample comes out of the discharge tubing. Turn the control valve horizontal, put the tubing back into the cylinder, and turn the valve vertical again so the sample circulates while you set the pressure. **The sample must contain no clumps**, or it will lodge in a check valve and stop the instrument.
7. **Set the pressure.** Gently pull the grey regulator knob (F) up to unlock it (an orange band is visible), then turn it. The pneumatic control gauge (N) should read about **40 psi**, and the homogenizing pressure gauge (O) should be stable at **20,000–25,000 psi**.
   - Clockwise: raises air pressure in the control cylinder, closes the gap, lowers the flow rate and raises the homogenizing pressure.
   - Counter-clockwise: lowers air pressure, opens the gap, raises the flow rate and lowers the homogenizing pressure.
8. When the sample comes out and you hear stable homogenization, collect it in a clear tube.
9. Leave just enough liquid to cover the inlet valve to avoid air. Pour the collected sample back in and **repeat 2–3 times** (closed cycle) to ensure complete homogenization.
10. After 2–3 cycles, turn the control valve horizontal and turn the regulator knob off to release pressure.

## Cleaning

1. Run about 100 mL of buffer through (repeat step 3), then about 250 mL of distilled water.
2. Add another 250 mL of distilled water, wait 10 min, then run it through.
3. Run about 50 mL of isopropanol through.
4. Fill the cylinder with isopropanol and close the cap (H).

## Precautions

- Do not run the instrument dry for more than a few cycles. Without liquid lubrication the plunger can seize in the pump, which shortens seal life.
- Run only clean fluids. Particles block the check valves.
- Do not load too much sample; it can block the check valves and stop the pump.
