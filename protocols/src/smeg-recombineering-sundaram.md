**This protocol is different from the one on the [recombineering page](recombineering.html), including the inducer, induction timing and cell handling. The two have not been reconciled.**

## Strains and plasmids

- *M. smegmatis* mc² wild type, with pJV53 (kanamycin; acetamide-inducible) or pNIT-recET-*sacB* (kanamycin).
- *M. smegmatis* Δ*recBCD* (unmarked) on 7H10.
- Δ*recBCD* carrying pJV53 (kanamycin) on 7H10 + kanamycin 25 µg/mL.
- Δ*recBCD* carrying pNIT-recET-*sacB*-Zeo on 7H10 + zeocin 25 µg/mL.
- Δ*recBCD* carrying pNIT-recET-*sacB*-Gent on 7H10 + gentamicin 10 µg/mL.

This protocol is written for hygromycin-marked recombineering constructs.

## Protocol

1. Keep kanamycin 25 µg/mL in the culture only until the preparation of competent cells.
2. Add the inducer between OD 0.65 and 0.8 and incubate with shaking for 3 hours: **caprolactam** for pNIT, **acetamide** for pJV53. Final inducer concentration is 0.2%.
3. Spin down and wash the culture with 10% glycerol 2–3 times (about 30–40 mL 10% glycerol for cells from a 25 mL culture).
4. Resuspend the pellet in about one-tenth the volume of 10% glycerol (a little more concentrated is fine). More concentrated cells (from 50 mL into 1–2 mL) work as long as there is no arcing. Test the cells first with a no-DNA transformation: the time constant should be above 20 ms; below 15 ms the cells will most likely arc when DNA is added.
5. Electroporate DNA into 400 µL of competent cells. After electroporation, transfer the cells to 4 mL 7H9 with no antibiotic (1 mL also works). Incubate with shaking at 37 °C for 3 hours.
6. Spin down the cells and plate on two 7H10 + hygromycin 50 µg/mL plates (no kanamycin at any stage after making competent cells). More concentrated competent cells may need more than two hygromycin plates to avoid background hygromycin-resistant colonies.
7. Pick 10–20 hygromycin-resistant colonies and restreak on 7H10 + hygromycin 50 µg/mL as small patches, enough for colony PCR and inoculation.
8. After 2–3 days of growth, screen the clones by colony PCR.
9. Grow 2–4 colony-PCR-positive clones in 7H9 + hygromycin 50 µg/mL for 2–3 days. Confirm again by PCR (2 µL of settled bacterial suspension in a 50 µL reaction).
10. Streak out each culture on 7H10 + 15% sucrose plates to get isolated colonies. Colonies may stay tiny for a while. Dilute the cultures and plate undiluted, 10⁻² and 10⁻⁴ to get well-isolated colonies. A clone may occasionally become spontaneously kanamycin resistant, so plate 3–4 independent clones.
11. Pick about 15–20 colonies per clone and streak first on kanamycin and then, **with the same tip held in the same position**, on hygromycin. This is critical; do not pick different inocula for the two plates. No more than 20 colonies per clone are needed. If no kanamycin-sensitive colonies appear in the first 15–20, discard that clone if others are available. Usually all, or more than 90%, of the colonies have lost the recET plasmid and are kanamycin sensitive; typically a kanamycin-sensitive colony appears within the first 6.
12. Pick the clone from the corresponding hygromycin plate, grow in 7H9 + hygromycin, and make frozen stocks.

## Notes from the source

- First try the Δ*recBCD* strain carrying pJV53. It works well in the author's hands and the plasmid is not hard to lose.
- If making cell-wall mutants in the Δ*recBCD* strain, compare your mutant phenotype with the parental unmarked Δ*recBCD* and with wild-type mc²155 to confirm that the *recBCD* mutation itself does not contribute. No difference in polar growth or division was found.
