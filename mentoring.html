Mentoring & Training

My goal is to help each trainee become a rigorous, independent scientist who can make sound decisions, contribute generously, and build a future that fits their own ambitions. I’ll adapt my guidance to your experience and goals while being clear about expectations, feedback, and support.

What you can expect from me

  • Clear expectations about project goals, communication, and how we will assess progress.
  • Regular check-ins to discuss research, obstacles, training needs, and career development—not just results.
  • Constructive feedback that is specific and actionable, with room for questions and disagreement.
  • Support that adapts as your skills and confidence grow: more structure when it helps, more autonomy when you are ready.
  • Respect for your privacy, well-being, and career choices, including paths outside academia.
  • Fair conversations about authorship, credit, opportunities, and project responsibilities.

What I’ll ask of you

  • Keep useful records and explain what you did, why you did it, and what the data can—and cannot—show.
  • Raise questions, delays, mistakes, and unexpected results early. You do not need to have a solution before asking for help.
  • Come to discussions prepared to share your reasoning, consider alternatives, and identify the next useful step.
  • Communicate respectfully, acknowledge others’ contributions, and contribute to a lab where people can disagree without humiliation.
  • Tell me when the mentoring approach is not working for you. We can discuss what needs to change.

How we’ll work together

Build independence gradually. You’ll practise evaluating evidence, comparing explanations, spotting weaknesses in experiments, and proposing ways to test competing hypotheses. I’ll guide the process without expecting you to agree with me automatically.

Make feedback useful. We’ll discuss what is working, what needs attention, and what concrete next step would help. If a problem persists, we’ll clarify expectations, agree on support and actions, and set a time to revisit progress.

Treat setbacks as information. Honest mistakes and confusing results are part of research. We’ll troubleshoot systematically and document changes. Repeated carelessness that affects others needs to be addressed; deliberate fabrication is incompatible with research.

Keep communication clear. We’ll discuss goals, timelines, responsibilities, and likely outputs early, then revisit them as projects evolve. Please flag delays or changing capacity so we can reset priorities and plan handoffs where needed.

Authorship, credit, and collaboration

We’ll talk about likely contributions and authorship early and revisit them as the work changes. Decisions should reflect meaningful intellectual and practical contributions, relevant journal and institutional guidance, and transparent discussion—especially when projects overlap. Technical, administrative, mentoring, and collaborative contributions should also be acknowledged appropriately.

Career development and sponsorship

We’ll revisit your career goals regularly and identify experiences, skills, relationships, and opportunities that support them. I’ll aim to help you build a network beyond the lab, make relevant introductions, recommend you for appropriate opportunities, and advocate for your work. Your future belongs to you; you do not need to pursue my career path to have my support.

Well-being, inclusion, and accountability

You are a whole person, not a data-generating machine. You should not have to disclose private details to be treated with respect. If your capacity changes, we’ll work together on realistic priorities and timelines while communicating clearly about work that affects others.

People should be able to ask questions, admit mistakes, raise concerns, and disagree without humiliation. Harmful conduct still needs to be addressed fairly, with attention to the facts, the people affected, and appropriate institutional processes. Scientific ability does not excuse behavior that undermines colleagues.
