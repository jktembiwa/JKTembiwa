Lab wiki · Keep learning

Resources & field notes.

Useful tools for doing careful science, thinking clearly, learning together, and making the day-to-day work of research a little easier.

This is a working collection, not a list of things everyone must use. Some links are practical tools; others are prompts for discussion or ideas to adapt. We’ll keep adding resources that are genuinely useful and note where context matters.

01 · Questions and evidence

Research & scientific thinking

Find the literature, follow ideas across papers, and keep the distinction between what the evidence shows and what we think it might mean.

Find and follow the research

  • PubMed — biomedical and life-sciences literature
  • Google Scholar — broad scholarly search and citation trails

Questions worth asking of a result

What do the controls establish? Which alternative explanations remain? Does an independent method support the same interpretation? What would change our mind? A result can be interesting and worth sharing before it is a settled conclusion—provided its limitations are clear.

02 · Bench to data

Methods & practical tools

A small toolbox for sequences, proteins, reagents, protocols, and publicly available high-throughput datasets.

Genes, genomes, and proteins

  • NCBI — sequence records, literature, and biological databases
  • UniProt — protein sequences and functional annotation
  • Addgene — plasmid repository and molecular biology guides

Protocols and public datasets

  • protocols.io — shareable, versioned research protocols
  • Sequence Read Archive (SRA) — high-throughput sequencing data
  • Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) — functional genomics datasets
03 · People and practice

Mentoring, teaching & lab leadership

Ideas for building a lab where scientific standards are high, expectations are explicit, and people can learn, contribute, and grow.

CIMER mentoring resources ↗

Evidence-informed approaches to mentoring relationships, conversations, and development. Use them as a starting point and adapt thoughtfully to the person and setting.

The CLEAR Lab Book ↗

A public living manual combining lab values, orientations, guidelines, and practical protocols. Especially useful as an example of documenting not only what a lab does, but how it works together.

“Starting CLEAR, maintaining CLEAR” ↗

A detailed reflection on starting and sustaining a lab, including lab structure, mentoring, decision-making, values, and the often-invisible work of running a research group. Its practices are situated examples, not a universal template.

Make expectations discussable

Mentoring agreements, individual development plans, regular feedback, transparent authorship conversations, and explicit meeting norms can turn assumptions into things a group can revisit and improve.

04 · Ideas to try

The genuinely useful stuff

Practical ideas, thoughtful writing, and small systems that might make research life more intentional. These are invitations to adapt—not policies by default.

A “State of the Lab” meeting ↗

A post you flagged as inspiration for a semesterly lab-wide reflection. A recurring pause to look at how the group is doing could complement project meetings by making room for shared priorities, working practices, and what needs to change.

Starting and maintaining a lab ↗

Keep this close as a prompt for future PI development: lab size, space, staffing, funding, decision-making, documentation, and the difference between the lab you imagine and the one you can sustainably support.

ORCID ↗

A persistent researcher identifier that helps distinguish your scholarly record across papers, institutions, and career transitions.

Keep this collection alive

If a resource saved time, clarified a hard decision, or helped someone learn a skill, add it with a sentence about when it is useful. If a link stops helping, we can retire it.

A note on context

Research tools and leadership approaches are not one-size-fits-all. Check current documentation, licensing, institutional requirements, and the evidence behind advice before applying it. The public wiki is for reusable guidance—not confidential records, unpublished results, or sensitive lab discussions.
