Methods · From mechanism to measurement

How I investigate bacterial biology.

My work combines genetics, biochemical mechanism, protein-interaction assays, microscopy, and quantitative analysis. The choice of method starts with the question—and with what evidence would make competing explanations distinguishable.

Methods follow the question

No single technique provides a complete explanation of a biological system. I aim to connect molecular hypotheses to measurable phenotypes, use controls that support the claims being made, and bring independent approaches together when a result carries substantial interpretive weight.

The methods below reflect my research training and experience; not every approach is used in every project.

How I work

Building an explanation from multiple kinds of evidence

My training has emphasized connecting molecular hypotheses to measurable phenotypes and being explicit about what each experiment can—and cannot—establish.

Genetics and perturbation

Allelic exchange, targeted and site-directed mutagenesis, CRISPRi, complementation, domain deletions, and phenotypic characterization to ask whether a component is necessary and how a change affects the system.

Biochemistry and enzymology

Recombinant protein expression and purification, in-vitro enzyme activity assays, and work with peptidoglycan synthases and hydrolases, kinases, phosphatases, and proteases to probe mechanism.

Protein interactions

Co-immunoprecipitation, mycobacterial protein-interaction assays, BioLayer Interferometry, and structural prediction—using complementary methods rather than treating one assay as definitive.

Microscopy and metabolic labeling

Live-cell confocal and epifluorescence microscopy, time-lapse imaging, fluorescent cell-wall probes, and metabolic labeling to examine where cellular processes occur.

Quantitative image analysis

Automated image segmentation and MicrobeJ, alongside R/RStudio and GraphPad Prism, to turn microscopy and experimental measurements into interpretable quantitative comparisons.

Physiology and antimicrobial response

Antimicrobial susceptibility measurements, including MIC and disk-diffusion approaches, to connect changes in cell biology with measurable outcomes while avoiding overinterpretation of any single phenotype.
