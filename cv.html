Research profile

Microbiologist and bacterial cell biologist investigating the molecular mechanisms that coordinate bacterial cell-envelope biosynthesis, growth, and physiology. My doctoral research combined bacterial genetics, protein biochemistry, fluorescence microscopy, and quantitative image analysis to study regulatory pathways controlling peptidoglycan and mycolic-acid synthesis in Mycobacterium smegmatis. My broader interests include bacterial physiology, antimicrobial response, microbial cell biology, and cell-to-cell variation.

Research experience

PgfA-mediated coordination of cell-envelope biosynthesis · 2024–2026

  • Investigated how PgfA connects polar peptidoglycan synthesis with mycolate synthesis in M. smegmatis.
  • Combined bacterial genetics, quantitative single-cell fluorescence microscopy, fluorescent metabolic probes, and chemical perturbations to study cell-envelope phenotypes.
  • Examined regulatory threonine phosphorylation sites in PgfA and their relationship to cell-wall organization and antibiotic susceptibility.

Regulatory architecture of the MurJ lipid II flippase · 2021–2024

  • Used CRISPRi, domain-deletion genetics, and complementary assays to examine MurJ regulation in mycobacteria.
  • Investigated a proposed MurJ–CwlM interaction using structural prediction, mycobacterial two-hybrid assays, and co-immunoprecipitation.

CwlM–MurA regulatory interface · Collaborative project, 2023–2024

  • Contributed to experimental design, molecular biology, protein purification, in-vitro enzyme assays, and interface-mutant characterization.

Technical skills

Bacterial molecular genetics: Allelic exchange, chromosomal mutagenesis, CRISPRi, genetic complementation, targeted and site-directed mutagenesis, molecular cloning, DNA/RNA extraction, PCR/qPCR, and phenotypic characterization.

Protein biochemistry and enzymology: Recombinant protein expression and purification, affinity and size-exclusion chromatography, enzyme assays, and protein-interaction analysis using BioLayer Interferometry, mycobacterial protein-fragment complementation, and co-immunoprecipitation.

Microscopy and image analysis: Live-cell confocal and epifluorescence microscopy, time-lapse imaging, fluorescent cell-wall labeling, automated image segmentation, and MicrobeJ.

Computational biology and data analysis: AlphaFold2/3, R/RStudio, GraphPad Prism, Geneious, Benchling, BLAST, STAR, and DESeq2.

Antimicrobial susceptibility: Minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) and disk-diffusion approaches.
