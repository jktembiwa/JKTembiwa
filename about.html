About Karen

Scientist, learner, collaborator.

I’m a microbiologist interested in how molecular mechanisms shape bacterial physiology—and how careful observation helps us understand biological complexity.

Current role

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at UiT The Arctic University of Norway, working with Dr. Christian Lentz.

Training and research background

I earned a PhD in Quantitative Biology from The University of Texas at Arlington in 2026. My doctoral research investigated the regulation of peptidoglycan synthesis and cell-wall assembly in Mycobacterium smegmatis, including work on the lipid II flippase MurJ and the polar growth factor PgfA.

My research experience includes bacterial genetics, biochemical and protein-interaction approaches, fluorescence and time-lapse microscopy, quantitative image analysis, and methods for investigating bacterial cell-envelope biology.

How I approach science

  • Curiosity: following interesting observations and asking questions that experiments can address.
  • Rigor: distinguishing results from interpretations and treating unexpected outcomes as information.
  • Shared learning: making scientific reasoning, methods, and resources easier to revisit.
  • Mentorship: supporting a research culture where people can ask questions, learn, and develop independence.
