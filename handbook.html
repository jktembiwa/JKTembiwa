Lab & individual policies.

Welcome to the lab. These are the working agreements I ask everyone to follow so we can do careful science, collaborate well, and respect one another’s time and well-being.

Communication

  • Please use our agreed shared channels for project decisions and information other people may need later.
  • When you ask for help, include enough context to explain the problem, your question, and how urgent it is.
  • I do not expect anyone to reply instantly outside agreed working hours. Please mark genuinely time-sensitive messages clearly.
  • Please summarize important decisions and action items in writing so everyone can find them, including colleagues who were not present.
  • Raise misunderstandings early when you can. If a conversation feels difficult or unsafe, you can ask me or an appropriate trusted person for support.

Meetings

  • When practical, I will share an agenda and materials before meetings; please do the same when you are leading one.
  • We will aim to finish meetings with clear decisions, responsibilities, and next steps.
  • Make room for questions, and do not assume everyone starts with the same background knowledge.
  • We can revisit recurring meetings if they stop being useful.

Authorship and credit

  • We will discuss likely outputs, project roles, and authorship expectations early, then revisit them as the work evolves.
  • Authorship should reflect meaningful scholarly contributions and the relevant journal or disciplinary criteria.
  • We will clarify responsibilities for data, analysis, writing, and submission, and resolve authorship questions before submission.
  • We will acknowledge technical, administrative, mentoring, and other contributions appropriately.
  • Follow applicable institutional and journal policies as well as our shared expectations.

Safety and responsible research

  • Complete required training and risk assessments before beginning work, and follow approved protocols and institutional biosafety procedures.
  • Ask before improvising with unfamiliar organisms, materials, equipment, or protocols.
  • Report incidents, near misses, exposures, equipment problems, and unsafe conditions promptly through the appropriate institutional channels.
  • Keep accurate records and protect confidential, personal, unpublished, and restricted information.
  • Store, back up, share, and retain data in line with institutional requirements and the project’s agreed plan.

Onboarding

  • At the start, we will clarify your role, supervisor, priorities, training requirements, access needs, and communication channels.
  • I will aim to provide a first-week plan, introductions, workspace orientation, and a named person you can ask about practical questions.
  • Before you work independently, we will confirm required safety and research-integrity training, equipment access, and approvals.
  • We will agree on initial check-ins and revisit goals once you have had time to settle in.

Offboarding and transitions

  • If you are leaving or changing projects, please plan a handover early enough to avoid losing important context.
  • Document project status, key decisions, methods, file locations, pending work, and relevant contacts.
  • Transfer data and materials only through approved channels and follow ownership, consent, privacy, and retention requirements.
  • Return equipment and access items, close accounts as directed, and clarify what may be shared or published.
  • We will make time for a respectful exit conversation and, where useful, record lessons that could improve the group.
