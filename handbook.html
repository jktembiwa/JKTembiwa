How we work.

A practical first draft of group working agreements. Specific procedures should be agreed with the team and aligned with institutional requirements before use.

Communication

  • Use shared, agreed channels for project decisions and information others will need later.
  • Include context, the question, and urgency when asking for help.
  • Do not expect instant replies outside agreed working hours; mark genuinely time-sensitive issues clearly.
  • Summarize important decisions and action items in writing so absent colleagues can catch up.
  • Raise misunderstandings early where possible; seek support when a conversation feels difficult or unsafe.

Meetings

  • Share an agenda and materials in advance when practical.
  • Finish with decisions, owners, and next steps.
  • Make space for questions and different levels of familiarity with a topic.
  • Review recurring meetings when they stop serving the team; protect focus time.

Authorship and credit

  • Discuss likely outputs, roles, and authorship expectations early, then revisit them as projects evolve.
  • Base authorship on meaningful scholarly contributions and relevant journal or disciplinary criteria, not seniority alone.
  • Agree on responsibilities for data, analysis, writing, and submission; document decisions and resolve uncertainty before submission.
  • Acknowledge technical, administrative, mentoring, and other contributions appropriately.
  • Follow applicable institutional and journal policies; these principles do not replace formal requirements.

Safety and responsible research

  • Complete required training and risk assessments before beginning work; follow approved protocols and institutional biosafety procedures.
  • Ask before improvising with unfamiliar organisms, materials, equipment, or procedures.
  • Report incidents, near misses, exposures, equipment problems, and unsafe conditions promptly through appropriate institutional channels.
  • Keep records accurate; protect confidential, personal, unpublished, and restricted information.
  • Plan data storage, backups, access, and retention with institutional requirements in mind.

Onboarding

  • Clarify the role, supervisor, priorities, training requirements, access needs, and communication channels before work begins.
  • Provide a first-week plan, introductions, workspace orientation, and a named person for practical questions.
  • Confirm required safety and research-integrity training, equipment access, and approvals before independent work.
  • Agree on initial check-ins and revisit goals once the person has context.

Offboarding and transitions

  • Plan a handover early enough to avoid last-minute loss of context.
  • Document project status, key decisions, methods, file locations, pending work, and contacts.
  • Transfer data and materials only through approved channels, following ownership, consent, privacy, and retention rules.
  • Return equipment and access items, close accounts as directed, and agree what can be shared or published.
  • Make time for a respectful exit conversation and capture lessons that can improve the group.

Reviewing these agreements

Review this handbook with team members at onboarding and periodically thereafter. Adapt it to local university requirements, funding conditions, professional guidance, and the needs of the actual group.
