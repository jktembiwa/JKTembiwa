Research

Questions that start small.

My background is in bacterial cell-envelope biology, with developing interests in microbial physiology and differences between individual cells.

Bacterial cell-envelope biology

My PhD research investigated the regulation of peptidoglycan synthesis and cell-wall assembly in Mycobacterium smegmatis, including work on the lipid II flippase MurJ and polar growth factor PgfA.

Doctoral research · University of Texas at Arlington

Variation between cells

A developing direction is to investigate how bacterial cells differ in physiological and enzymatic states, what molecular processes drive those differences, and whether they relate to stress responses or antibiotic outcomes.

Developing research interests

Methods I’ve worked with

Bacterial genetics, biochemical approaches, protein-interaction assays, fluorescence and time-lapse microscopy, quantitative image analysis, and metabolic labeling.

Questions that travel

Across systems, I’m interested in how molecular mechanisms and environmental context shape microbial behavior—and how to test those ideas carefully.
